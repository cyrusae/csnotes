# Questions for instruction-writing

Questions for the instruction files — these are the choices only you can make:

  ---
  1. Granularity of atomic notes
  When you learn that "a red-black tree is a BST where every path from root to null has the same number of black nodes, insertions rebalance
  via rotations and recoloring," is that one atomic note or several? Where's your instinct on how fine to cut things?
  
  2. What's worth a note at all
  Do you want the AI to synthesise everything from the lecture, or only things that feel like stable, reusable knowledge? E.g. would a worked
  example from class get its own note, or is it just evidence for a concept note?

> I think a worked example from class can stay in my notes and be evidence for a concept note until proven otherwise. Factual knowledge with a reference to "(worked example in class-mm-day lecture)".

  3. How you want uncertainty handled
  The AI will sometimes be unsure whether it understood your handwriting / shorthand correctly, or whether two things you wrote are the same
  concept under different names. Do you want it to ask via flags and leave a placeholder, take its best guess and flag it, or just quietly do
  its best?

> Do its best guess and flag it, I think? Hopefully it would come up in conversation before time came to write notes?

  4. Connection-making
  If the lecture introduces something that clearly relates to a concept from a previous session (or your textbook), should the AI actively
  weave those connections into the note body, add wikilinks, or just record what the new session said and leave the synthesis to a future study
  session?

> Actively make connections and add wikilinks

  5. Your voice vs. neutral
  Do you want the notes written in a neutral encyclopedic style ("Polymorphism is..."), in your voice as a learner ("Key insight: polymorphism lets you..."), or something else? Does it matter to you?

> Definitely not neutral encocylopedic style, I find conversational more engaging; I want it to err towards conversational-informal, like debriefing to a friend, and keep any stupid jokes I make intact

  6. Index notes — table of contents or more?
  Is the index note for a topic just a structured list of embeds, or do you want it to carry its own prose — an overview, a narrative thread
  connecting the atomics?

> I'm torn on this one, argue me the pros/cons of each option?
